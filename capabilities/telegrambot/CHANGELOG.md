# telegrambot — change log

## 2026-11-22 — `telegrambot help` is the menu, and `telegrambot help <verb>` one verb's whole contract

`telegrambot help` printed the whole reference, about 29.9 KB with flags on 55 of its lines, and `telegrambot help <anything>`, an unknown word included, was refused by the parser with exit 6 invalid_arguments. It is now the short complete menu of SHEBANG.md, "Agent-first help is the surface", about 6.7 KB: what the capability is and what it refuses, what a connection holds, chats and topics, every verb on one line saying what it answers - readiness, seen, history, download, the writes, prompts, profiles, jobs, the worker's `progress` and the service verbs - the I/O and the exit codes, then the contract verbs the library renders, with no flag on any line. `telegrambot help <verb>` prints that verb's whole contract from `HELP_VERBS` - its synopsis with every flag, the text the old help held for it, its output, exit codes and an example - for `seen`, `history`, `download`, `send`, `react`, `prompts list|show|put|history|retire`, `profiles list|show|save`, `jobs help|list|active|show|register|amend|submit|stop|resume|discard`, `progress` (the worker wrapper inside a turn or a job), and `service run|stop|reload|status|logs|doctor|harness`. CONNECTIONS moved to `help connections` and `help connection`, after the library's pages; the doctor text to `help doctor`; SETTINGS to `help settings`, after the library's page; the shared Deepgram key to `help setup`; the SERVICE section - how a message is admitted and answered, the prompt order, voice, chat commands, roles and the reply gate - to `help service run`. The old contract verbs list is the library's, and the library's declared connection, setup, settings and secret-cascade sections are now the pages of those verbs. `telegrambot help jobs`, `help prompts`, `help profiles` and `help service` list their verbs. `telegrambot --connection <id> help` prints the same menu as bare `telegrambot help`, and `--connection <id> help <verb>` reaches the verb's page. An unknown path exits 6 `unknown_verb` with nothing on stdout and the hint to run `telegrambot help`. The capability binds `capabilities-contract` 0.50 or later, which dispatches the layered help. No verb, flag, answer, JSON key or exit code changes.

## 2026-11-19 — A role can read named neighbouring groups and topics; writing stays in its own chat

`roles.<role>.read_chats` gives a role a read reach: `{"<group id>": {"topics": [<topic id>, ...]}}`, each group named explicitly, `topics` narrowing it to those topics (without it, the whole group). A turn or job for a sender of that role may then read history (`telegrambot history <chat> [--topic ID]`) and download files (`telegrambot download <chat> <message id>`) there, through the worker's `telegrambot` and by the CLI's own path alike; a download of a message outside the named topics exits 4 `topic_scope_denied`. The turn's channel state names the reach and its commands. `send`, `react` and `jobs` stay on the turn's own chat and topic whatever the reach says. A role with no `read_chats` - every role until one is set - works exactly as before: another chat exits 4 `chat_scope_denied`, another topic 4 `topic_scope_denied`. No existing verb, flag or output key changes.

## 2026-11-17 — Turns run on callva-harness-runner 0.12.3

Turns run on callva-harness-runner 0.12.3, in place of 0.12.2: finding the claude or codex executable no longer lists the fallback folders when `PATH` already has it. On a machine where fnm had left tens of thousands of per-shell folders, every turn's start spent over a second of CPU holding the interpreter on that list; it now takes well under a millisecond, and finds the same executable. Nothing a profile or a result does changes; no verb, flag or JSON key changes.

## 2026-11-16 — `telegrambot` declares its title and summary in Russian and Estonian

The manifest carries `translations`, from the `TRANSLATIONS` constant: the title and summary in Russian (`ru`), Telegram-бот, and Estonian (`et`), Telegrami bot, each as `title` and `description`. English `title` and `summary` stay the source and the fallback. The capability binds `capabilities-contract` 0.49 or later, which emits them. Nothing else changes.

## 2026-11-16 — A handed-off job's brief no longer reaches the chat

When a turn handed work to a background job and had no reply of its own - always so when the turn was cut short after its grace - the service posted the job's description, prefixed with ▶, as the bot's message. That description is the job worker's internal brief, written for the worker rather than for the people in the chat. The service now says only the turn's own reply; with none, it marks the request message with a ✍ reaction, as it already does for a message added to a job, and a reply to that message reaches the job. The job's result is still the only answer it posts. No verb, flag, setting or output key changes.

## 2026-11-10 — A message about a running job reaches the job, and the job answers once

A message that arrived while one of its chat's background jobs ran started a turn of its own beside the job, which knew nothing of the job unless the model asked the register, so the bot could answer the same subject twice. The service now gives every turn the chat's or topic's waiting and running jobs in `Channel state` (`Open jobs here:`, each job's short id, state, who asked, the message it came from and its outcome line). A reply from a job's requester to the message the job came from, or to a line the service sent for the job, is added to that job as an amendment without a turn - a running job continues its session with it, a waiting one starts with it - and is marked with a ✍ reaction. A turn that adds to a job through its own `jobs amend` is ended like a turn that hands work over: its short line stands, nothing else it would say is sent, and a turn cut short leaves a ✍ instead; an amendment made anywhere else - another topic's turn, the operator's CLI, the service's own routing - ends no turn. Any other message is answered while the job runs, as before. No verb, flag, setting or output key changes.

## 2026-11-10 — `telegrambot` declares its title, Telegram Bot, and its Lucide icon, `bot-message-square`

The manifest carries `title`, `Telegram Bot`, the capability's human name, fixed here and never renamed, and `lucide_icon`, `bot-message-square`, the Lucide name of its line icon, from the `TITLE` and `LUCIDE_ICON` constants, so a reader draws the icon as a vector at its own weight and size. The capability binds `capabilities-contract` 0.48 or later, which emits them. It is still addressed as `telegrambot`. Nothing else changes.

## 2026-11-10 — Inside a turn the CLI reaches the turn's chat only, by whichever path it is called

Inside a turn the service started (`TELEGRAMBOT_TURN` set), `history`, `download`, `react` and `send` now hold the turn's chat whether they are called through the worker's `telegrambot` or by the CLI's own path: a call naming another chat exits 4 `chat_scope_denied`, and `history` or `send` naming another topic of the turn's chat exits 4 `topic_scope_denied`, before anything reaches Telegram. Before, only the worker's `telegrambot` held that line, and a turn calling the CLI by its path could read or write any chat the bot is in. A call for the turn's own chat and topic, and every call outside a turn, works as before. No verb, flag or output key changes.

## 2026-11-10 — No job is entered for an archived agent, at telegrambot 2.2

Step `0009-unarchived` (telegrambot 2.2, a minor step needing the store's core at 4.2, capabilities-contract 0.47) has the store refuse every insert of `telegrambot_jobs` for an archived agent, through the core's one guard `agents_archived_refuse`, with SQLSTATE 23R01; moving and ending a job stay allowed. `jobs register` that meets it exits 4 `agent_archived`. A release of telegrambot 2.1 keeps working against a store at 2.2 for every agent that is not archived. Declares `capabilities-contract>=0.47,<1`. No verb, flag or JSON key changes.

## 2026-11-08 — Channels, jobs and prompts go with their agent, at telegrambot 2.1

Step `0008-keys` (telegrambot 2.1, core 4.0) keys the channels, seen marks, jobs, job slots, job amendments and prompts to their agent (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), a job's slots and amendments to their job, and a job keeps its row and loses the node it ran on where that goes (`ON DELETE SET NULL`). Runs on capabilities-contract 0.45 and callva-harness-runner 0.12.0. No verb, flag or JSON key changes.

## 2026-11-08 — Replies reach Telegram formatted, not as raw Markdown

What the service sends for a turn - the answer, its progress lines, what the turn or a job sends into the chat, a file's caption and a job's result - reached the chat as the model wrote it, `**` and backticks included. The transport now renders that Markdown into Telegram's message entities and sends them with no parse mode, so no character needs escaping: bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, fenced code blocks (with their language), links, quotes and flat lists render; a heading becomes a bold line, a table a code block as written, and a list item a `•` line. Text that is not markup - `2*3*4`, `snake_case`, `<tag> &` - arrives whole, and a backslash keeps a marker literal. A reply is cut at 4096 UTF-16 units, as Telegram counts them, and an entity across a cut continues in the next part. A part whose formatting Telegram refuses is sent once more as the same text without it, so a reply is never lost to its formatting. The shipped turn and job mechanics name the forms Telegram renders and ask for no headings, tables or nested lists. The voice echo and the `send` verb still send text as written. No verb, flag, environment variable or output key changes.

## 2026-11-08 — A sender's role can name the profile their turn runs on

A role can now name its own harness profile under the new settings key `roles.<role>.profile`, validated like every other profile key; `profiles list` names it at that position, and `service harness` counts it among the profiles a turn may run on. A turn from a sender whose role names one runs on that profile, in place of the chat's or topic's and over a chat's `/set profile`, so ordinary group members can run on a read-only profile while the supervisor's turns keep the chat's. A role that names none runs on the chat's or topic's profile as before, and background jobs keep `job_profile`. The profile's sandbox and tools are the profile's own; nothing else about a turn changes. No verb, flag, environment variable or output key changes.

## 2026-11-07 — A connection is named by its id: telegrambot's tables go to schema major 2

A connection - a bot - is keyed by the opaque id the store gives it, and called by its label; `--connection` takes either. Step `0007-connection-ids` (schema 2.0, reading the core at 3.0) gives `telegrambot_channels`, `telegrambot_seen`, `telegrambot_jobs`, `telegrambot_job_amendments` and `telegrambot_job_slots` two columns in place of `connection`, exactly one of them set: `connection_id`, the id of a bot the store holds, with a foreign key that removes the rows with their connection, and `file_connection`, the id of a bot only files hold - a files-mode project, or a machine's own connection lent to a store-mode project - whose rows keep working as before. Every key and index takes whichever is set where the name was, and a write puts the id in `connection_id` where the store holds that connection, else in `file_connection`. A name the store's map of the cutover resolves takes its id; any other stays, unchanged, a files connection's. Every case over the convenient case: the shape of a node's `coolify_connection_id` and `coolify_address`. The step crosses only through `capabilities store upgrade`; outside it a store at major 1 is refused with `store_upgrade_needed`. A bot's folder made under its connection's former name, its MTProto session included, is moved to `<state>/<id>` the first time the bot is used, saying so on stderr, so history reads sign in with the session they had. `jobs list` and `jobs active` still answer `connection` on each job, now the id. Declares `capabilities-contract>=0.44,<1`. No verb, flag, environment variable or output key changes.

`inventory` names each bot by its connection's label, where it named the id.

`doctor` and `service doctor` name each bot by its connection's label: what a bot lacks, why it is not served (`skipped`, keyed by label), its row under `bots` and each problem line, where each named the id.

## 2026-11-05 — The harness profiles are `harness_profile_versions`, behind the store upgrade

telegrambot migrates the harness runner's profile table before its own, so it now pins `callva-harness-runner` 0.11.0, whose step takes that table to schema major 2 as `harness_profile_versions` with the agent in `agent_id`, and declares `capabilities-contract>=0.43,<1`, the library that reads the store's core tables by their new names. The move crosses only through `capabilities store upgrade`, which migrates the harness owner through telegrambot as its further owner; outside it, telegrambot refuses a store still at schema major 1 with `store_upgrade_needed`, naming that command. telegrambot's own tables, which already name the agent `agent_id`, stay at 1.0, and `telegrambot_prompts` keeps its name. Profiles resolve and save as before. No verb, flag, environment variable or output key changes.

## 2026-11-05 — History reads a supergroup the bot is in

`history`, and a turn's read back, refused every supergroup with `chat_unreachable (ChannelInvalidError)`: the read named the channel with access hash 0, which `channels.getMessages` does not accept. The reader now asks `channels.getChannels` for the channel first, which accepts hash 0 from a bot that is a member, takes the access hash Telegram answers with, and keeps it for as long as the reader lives, so a long-running service asks once per supergroup. A supergroup the bot is not in is still refused as `chat_unreachable`; private chats and basic groups read as before.

## 2026-11-04 — An agent's own Telegram channel through a bot

New capability. One connection is one bot and holds everything that bot needs - the BotFather token, the App api_id and api_hash that let it read history, and the Deepgram key for voice - each as a value sealed into the agent's vault or a reference to a key held there, so a connection is whole on any machine. A new connection refers to the machine's shared `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY` where the machine holds one; nothing else stands in for a key the connection does not name.

New messages arrive and every write leaves over the HTTP Bot API; history is read back from Telegram by message number through an MTProto session the bot's own token signs in. Nothing keeps a copy of the conversation. `doctor` reports the bot, its privacy mode, a webhook that would stop polling, history readiness and voice readiness, and names what a connection lacks. `seen` lists the chats, topics and people the bot has seen; `history`, `download`, `send` and `react` act on one chat; `prompts` keeps the agent's own prompt over the shipped standard; `profiles` and `jobs` reach the harness profiles and the background work.

The bundled service runs once per machine for every project that joins it, or in one project: one getUpdates poll per bot, direct chats, groups and forum topics, one person's messages within the debounce as one turn, roles for commands and for tools, harness profiles, background jobs, the reply gate in shadow or live mode, voice transcription with a per-chat echo switch, `/status`, `/stop`, `/stop N`, `/profiles`, `/reload` and `/set`, and a bound on turns other bots start in a row, on by default. Its state is the project's store: `telegrambot_channels`, `telegrambot_seen`, `telegrambot_prompts` and the job tables.
